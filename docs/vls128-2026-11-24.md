## What is new about this sensor

- **128 beams, organised cloud** (`x,y,z,intensity float32, ring uint16, time
  float32`, 22 bytes/point, `width 128, height ~1900`), `/lidar_cc/velodyne_points`
  at 10 Hz. Much denser than the previous rig and the road geometry is clean.
- **8 pano cameras** (`camera_pano_{nn,ne,ee,se,ss,sw,ww,nw}`) at 1280×1024,
  ~20 Hz. We keep the six that map onto the nuScenes slots
  (`nn,ne,nw,ss,sw,se` → `FRONT,FRONT_RIGHT,FRONT_LEFT,BACK,BACK_LEFT,BACK_RIGHT`);
  `ee`/`ww` (pure left/right, ±90°) are unused, which leaves a gap at exactly
  ±90° that the old rig did not have.
- The sensor sits **~2 m above the road** (ground plane at z ≈ −1.3 … −2.2 m in
  the LiDAR frame, and the offset differs per recording), so any code that
  assumes a fixed ground height must use a per-frame/per-cell ground model.

## Geometry is correct — verified three ways

This matters because the folder is labelled "uncalibrated" and word of a "50°
offset" was going around. It is not in the geometry:

1. **LiDAR → camera projection** lands exactly on the scene (road surface,
   bridge, building edges) for all six cameras.
2. **Pano camera azimuths** are sane: front at −0.8°, FRONT_RIGHT −45°,
   FRONT_LEFT +44°, BACK 180°, BACK_LEFT 135°, BACK_RIGHT −136°.
3. **Reported yaw vs heading from motion** agrees to ±2° on straight sections
   (the only large deviations are frames that straddle an actual turn).

So the 50° claim, if it came from a map overlay, was measuring **map coverage**,
not a rotation: 21 of 43 candidate frames are 30-400 m away from the nearest
mapped lanelet, and on the frames that *are* on mapped lanes the map's hand-drawn
lane boundaries are 1-3 m off the real paint. No rotation fits better than any
other.

## What looks like lane lines is the beam-ring footprint

This is the single most misleading thing about a BEV rendering of this sensor,
so it is worth stating plainly.

A top-down view of the ground band is full of long, dense, evenly spaced
parallel lines that look exactly like road markings. They are not. They are the
**128 beams intersecting the road plane**: concentric arcs centred on the
sensor, spaced ~1.4 m apart at close range and tightening with distance. The
spacing is the giveaway.

Measured on `vls24_ringday_f0/frame_0000`: the histogram of range for on-ground
returns peaks at **4.5, 6.0, 7.3, 8.8, 10.1 m …** — a constant ~1.4 m step. Real
lane paint would be 3-4 m apart, would not be concentric, and would not appear
on grass and in car parks, where these rings also appear.

Practical test in any viewer: colour the cloud by **range from the sensor**. If
the "lane lines" become constant-radius rings, they are the beam pattern.

## The "green tinted lines" in RViz are the brightest beam rings

Colouring the cloud by intensity and boosting contrast, the most striking
features on the ground are a few bright green/cyan arcs. They are **real bright
returns**, and they are still rings — just the rings where the beam hits the
surface at the most grazing angle and comes back strongest.

Measured on three frames: take ground-band returns and keep only those above an
intensity threshold, then histogram range.

| frame | threshold | n | % of points within 7 cm of a few discrete radii |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ringday_f0/frame_0000` | I>30 | 522 | **64 %** |
| `ringday_f0/frame_0000` | I>35 | 417 | **73 %** |
| `ringday_f1/frame_0003` | I>25 | 381 | **80 %** |
| `ringday_f1/frame_0003` | I>30 | 235 | **97 %** |

The bright returns collapse onto a handful of radii — 6.1, 15.6-16.0,
18.5-18.9 m — i.e. constant range from the sensor. That is a ring, not a
painted line: paint follows the road, while these follow the beam geometry, and
they appear on every surface.

**Consequence for anyone reading this cloud:** the dominant intensity variation
on the ground here is *incidence angle*, not surface material. Any threshold
placed on raw intensity selects a range annulus, which renders as a convincing
"lane line" in a BEV or a 3-D view. Normalise per range before thresholding, or
detect in range-azimuth space.

## Intensity carries only a weak, unreliable paint signal

I first concluded there was *none*; that was too strong. Measured properly —
camera-derived paint mask (morphological top-hat on the road corridor), every
LiDAR return projected and labelled, then AUC of LiDAR intensity as the paint
classifier — across 44 frames:

| statistic | value |
|---|---|
| mean AUC | **0.45** |
| median AUC | 0.43 |
| best frame | **0.76** (`vls24_ringday_f0/frame_0000`, 239 paint points, I 35 vs 16) |

So on a good frame paint *is* roughly 2x brighter than asphalt, but on most
frames the channel is at or below chance. Compare the single clean pair measured
earlier (white dashed line vs asphalt at the same range): 22 vs 23.

Two reasons the signal is unusable as a threshold today: the reflectivity
calibration is not applied (the folder says "Uncalibrated"), and the ring
footprint dominates the point budget near the ego, so any fixed threshold
selects rings, verges and snow rather than paint.

**What to do.** If a reliable cloud-only lane extractor is the goal, the order is
(1) get the reflectivity calibration, (2) detect in **range-azimuth space**
where rings are straight lines at constant range and paint is an isolated
anomaly crossing azimuth, (3) only then Hough/ridge-fit polylines. Doing (3)
first, as `scripts/lane_from_lidar.py` does, produces the fragments visible in
the repo's overlay images — correct pipeline, no signal.


## Full-bag scan (2026-11-25)

Rather than sampling, every LiDAR message in four complete ring-road bags
(~85 min of driving, 842 scored frames) was walked with `scan_bag.py`: per-cell
ground model, per-range intensity baseline, then a test for bright ground
returns whose radial support is too low to be a ring.

No frame produced lane-like structure. The highest-scoring frame is the decisive
test case, because its camera shows a **crosswalk, four painted yield triangles
and a yellow centre line** within 20 m. Its ground-band cloud at 0.04 m/px
contains only the ring family — no triangles, no zebra bars, no centre line
(`fullscan_bestframe_ground_20m.png` next to `fullscan_bestframe_camera.png` in
`~/wato/media/lane_diagnostics/`).

So the "lane lines" in a BEV of this sensor are the beam footprint, confirmed on
a frame where a real painted shape would be unmissable.

### Why paint is missing: it falls between the beams

The ground cloud is not merely low-contrast — it is **under-sampled**. Projecting
one frame's returns onto its own camera image (`PROOF_cloud_projected_on_camera.png`)
shows the ground returns as discrete rows crossing the road, and the painted
yield triangles and zebra stripes visible in that same image sit *between* rows:
the beams never touch them.

Numbers for that frame: ~20,900 ground returns inside a 40x40 m box (13 / m^2),
but concentrated into rows roughly 0.4-0.5 m apart at 8 m, growing with range.
A lane stripe is 0.10-0.15 m wide, so a single scan samples it at a handful of
points — not enough to resolve a line, and not enough for a 1 m triangle to
survive as a shape.

This is the same reason nuScenes uses **10 accumulated sweeps**: ego motion
shifts the beam pattern each sweep and fills the gaps. Our extraction keeps one
scan, so paint is structurally invisible in it regardless of intensity
calibration. Accumulating 5-10 sweeps and then re-running this scan is the next
test, and it is the one that could still prove the point in favour of the
point-cloud approach.

## Limitations

1. **Night session not labelled.** `vls24_ringnight` files were still being
   ingested when this branch was cut (0 frames so far). Re-run steps 1-2 for
   those four files and append the masks — it is the only night data we have.
2. **Only 32 of 53 ingested frames are inside the lanelet map.** The drives leave the
   mapped campus/ring-road area (King St east and the north end of the ring are
   outside `Waterloo_Region.osm` too). Those frames have no lanelet GT and are
   not in `masks/`.
3. **No vector GT for these sessions.** `vector_gt/vector_gt.jsonl` is unchanged;
   the new frames so far only have raster masks. `export_vector_gt.py` can fill
   this in from the same manifest.
4. **Map geometry is coarse.** The divider/lane-boundary labels are 1-3 m off the
   real paint on these newer, wider roads (measured by fitting the map boundaries
   to LiDAR paint candidates: residual 2-7 m, best-fit yaw unstable across
   frames). Treat `divider` here as a weak label.
